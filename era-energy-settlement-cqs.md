## 1. Method

The CQs were produced and validated with current practice in ontology requirements engineering:

- **LOT methodology** (Poveda-Villalón et al., 2022). Requirements specification produces functional requirements as CQs grouped by theme, each traced to its source. Validation turns each CQ into a test run against the implemented ontology, and failing CQs feed the next iteration.
- **Competency questions** (Grüninger and Fox, 1995) written from the documents LOT treats as domain sources: the ERA AS-03 study, TEL TSI Annex 2.5.1(16) and 2.5.2(8), ENE TSI Art. 9, EN 50463 and UIC IRS 90930.
- **User stories per set.** Each set starts from a stakeholder need (IM, RU, settlement operator, Eress, ERA, regulatory body), as LOT recommends for collaborative requirement elicitation.
- **CQ archetypes** (Ren et al., 2014) and the **CLaRO** controlled language (Keet et al., 2019). Every CQ is tagged with its pattern (selection, counting, aggregation, existence, taxonomy, negation, temporal), which shows the requirement types are balanced.
- **CQs as executable tests.** Each CQ is translated into SPARQL with an expected answer, as in the CQ-to-SPARQL-OWL dataset (Potoniec et al., 2020). TBox CQs play the role of Themis-style requirement tests (Fernández-Izquierdo and García-Castro, 2019) checking axioms; ABox CQs check the model answers real questions over example data.
- **SHACL** checks normalized EVN matching, measurement periods, allocation structure, run/message identity, exchange evidence fields, and evidence requirements for late composition messages. Python aggregation checks allocation-component totals and that components do not over-allocate source readings.
- **LLM-assisted drafting with expert review.** The CQs were drafted with an LLM, an approach studied in OntoChat (Zhang et al., 2024). They need review by domain experts (Eress, Bane NOR) before the requirements are frozen.

## 4. Open requirements and unverified claims

These questions have not been settled by this proposal. Some now have model fields, but cannot be answered reliably for an operational or legal case without authoritative source data and expert-verified rules.

| ID | Competency question | Why it fails | Proposed action |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Was a composition message sent after departure justified by an anomaly? | The draft records a timing assessment, evidence reference, and authority reference, but the cited rule, allowed window, and meaning of a qualifying anomaly have not been verified against the official instrument. | Verify the operative TEL TSI text and message schema with ERA/legal experts; use a controlled outcome vocabulary and evidence mapping without treating a populated link as proof of compliance. |
| G2 | What was the train's actual departure time? | ens:actualDepartureTime now distinguishes actual from booked time and requires an evidence reference; no authoritative departure event or interface mapping is validated by the synthetic example. | Map an authoritative operating event and its provenance for each deployment; do not infer departure from the first location report without an approved operational rule. |
| G3 | Which energy supplier delivered the allocated energy, and at what price? | Supplier, contract, and price data are outside this iteration and must not be inferred from settlement party or infrastructure operator. | Add a separate market/contract module after verifying the operative legal interface obligations and responsible actors. |
| G4 | How were network losses allocated between consumers? | Loss coefficients and their allocation (AS-03 §5.6.2(g), Infrabel 96 % rule) are not modelled. | Add a loss allocation method concept and coefficient to ens:EnergyAllocation. |
| G5 | In which unit is the train weight of a composition message expressed? | era:trainWeight (xsd:int) has no era:unitOfMeasure in rc.3. | Raise with ERA (TAF practice: tonnes). |
| G6 | Which legal, privacy, security, and retention controls govern each exchange? | The exchange record now links a purpose, authority reference, recipient, outcome, and retention-policy reference, but example references are explicit placeholders and access controls are external to RDF. | Have the controller/processor roles, legal basis, purpose limitation, recipient authorization, retention/deletion schedule, security controls, and audit needs approved for each deployment. |

This list distinguishes data-model coverage from evidence quality: a populated evidence link is not proof that a legal condition is satisfied. Outstanding items need authoritative source or deployment decisions before production settlement use.

## 5. Notes on the tests

- **CQs run without a reasoner.** Subclass and subproperty entailments are written as property paths (`rdf:type/rdfs:subClassOf*`, `rdfs:subPropertyOf*`). CQ7.2 uses `ens:exchangesWith|^ens:exchangesWith` because the symmetry of the property is only entailed by a reasoner.
- **OWL 2 DL consistency is checked with HermiT** (`dl_check.py`) on the ERA snapshot, the extension, the examples and the vendored W3C SOSA, SSN and SSN-Ext files. Two fixtures prove that it detects an inconsistent ABox (two location reports on one running message, against the functional `era:trainLocationReport`) and an unsatisfiable class (a subclass of the disjoint `era:Body` and `era:OrganisationRole`).
  Inputs adjusted for OWL 2 DL before reasoning: removed 2 owl:imports; dropped SSN-Ext max-cardinality restriction on sosa:usedProcedure; cast xsd:minInclusive 0.0 to xsd:double (restriction of http://data.europa.eu/949/dNvroll); cast xsd:maxInclusive 327670.0 to xsd:double (restriction of http://data.europa.eu/949/dNvroll); removed owl:FunctionalProperty from ERA super-properties shared by several sub-properties: <http://data.europa.eu/949/companyCode>, <http://data.europa.eu/949/countryIdentISO>, <http://data.europa.eu/949/freeText>, <http://data.europa.eu/949/numberOfPassengerType>, <http://data.europa.eu/949/trackDataParameter>. HermiT runs with `--ignoreUnsupportedDatatypes` because `xsd:date` is outside the OWL 2 datatype map.
- **CQ11.2 depends on a SHACL rule.** The link from the composition journey section's normalized `era:locoNumber` to a registered vehicle is derived only when it matches a valid 12-digit vehicle number.
- **Readings are SOSA observation collections.** The source data states each energy value as a member `sosa:Observation` with a QUDT result. The compact `ens:activeEnergyConsumed`, `ens:activeEnergyRegenerated` and `ens:reactiveEnergy` values used by the S3, S4 and S13 questions are derived from those observations by the `enssh:EnergyReadingShape` rule.
- **Allocation arithmetic checks** verify component totals and cap aggregate component values at each source reading. The energy quantities are not prices and do not define market, contract, tax, or network-loss treatment.
- **Legal and privacy caveat:** exchange authority and retention references in examples are placeholders. The shapes validate reference presence, not legal validity, a GDPR basis, controller/processor allocation, access control, or retention compliance.
- **Example data is illustrative.** Organisation names are real; EVNs, train numbers, energy values, the border point ID and the energy supply concept are not.
- **To re-run:** `python3 cq_suite.py --md era-energy-settlement-cqs.md` (uses the vendored ERA ontology snapshot; needs the packages in `requirements.txt`).
